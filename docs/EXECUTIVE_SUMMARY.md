# Executive Summary

## Decision

Do not manage the 2024 complaint portfolio from the 99.72% headline timely-response rate alone. Prioritize the few product, issue, and company-product queues that account for most of the 7,737 untimely responses, while planning for a complaint volume that more than doubled from January to December.

## Evidence

- Volume increased from 143,365 complaints in January to 303,981 in December (+112.0%).
- Timeliness improved from 99.54% in January to 99.78% in December despite the higher workload.
- Credit reporting generated 2,365,586 complaints (86.5% of total) but only a 0.08% untimely rate; it is primarily a scale-management problem.
- Debt collection produced 2,708 untimely responses and a 1.74% untimely rate, making it a larger operational exception queue.
- Student loans produced 826 untimely responses and a 5.64% untimely rate, indicating a smaller but more acute service-risk pocket.
- Four issue queues accounted for 3,401 untimely responses (44.0%): debt-not-owed collection attempts, incorrect credit-report information, improper credit-report use, and student-loan lender/servicer problems.
- The largest eligible company-product exception queue was EdFinancial Services / Student loan: 1,114 complaints, 751 untimely responses, and a 32.59% timely rate.

## Recommended actions

1. Start root-cause review with the highest **untimely-count** queues, not simply the highest complaint volume.
2. Separate capacity management for credit reporting from control remediation in student loan, prepaid card, and debt-collection exceptions.
3. Review EdFinancial Services / Student loan, CCS Financial Services / Debt collection, and Incomm Holdings / Prepaid card first; validate data and operating context before escalation.
4. Use the Power BI target-rate parameter to set a service objective and quantify how many responses must shift from untimely to timely in the selected segment.
5. Add internal staffing, SLA-stage, ownership, and market-exposure data before using this work for formal performance management.

## Guardrail

CFPB complaint records are public submissions, not a statistical sample. High counts can reflect market size, customer base, product mix, access to complaint channels, or other exposure. Findings identify where to investigate; they do not establish wrongdoing or causality.
